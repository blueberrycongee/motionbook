# Independent blind visual review

Initial visual preferences frozen 2026-10-08, before opening results.json: **composer C > A > E**, with C/A close; **picker D > F > B**, with B's placement lower-confidence because its nested surface is not exposed in the supplied captures. These are relative preferences for these six studies, not universal scores or evidence about an unidentified condition.

No condition assignments, implementation notes, variant source, parent conclusions, or other judges' conclusions were inspected. The review uses the two briefs, JUDGING.md, reference contact sheets and README, actual Chromium screenshots at 960×720 and 390×720, and frame samples of the six 390 px no-preference recordings. Screenshot filenames contain runner status words; these were visible, but no runner result details were read before this preference freeze.

Further observations and a separate runner cross-check will be appended below; this preference statement will not be rewritten.

## Composer observations

- **C, preferred narrowly:** The largest usable editing area relative to its shell, clear green focus border, a compact context header, and bottom-row controls make the expanded composer easiest to read as one coherent component. The off-white document, green-gray background, fine border, restrained shadow, and serif document/sans-serif controls are consistent. At 390 px, the expanded composer sits directly below the document without horizontal crowding. At 960 px it overlaps the document's lower/footer area; that is compatible with a floating composer, but A preserves that footer better. The large blank paper area and very small, faint auxiliary captions are weaknesses. The preference for C's quieter hierarchy over A's extra labels is partly taste.
- **A, close second:** Strongest desktop edge alignment: paper and expanded composer share their outside edges. The shorter paper preserves separation from the expanded editor; the round shell, slight shadow, and square-ish inner text field are well related. The tiny document-context icon and focused toggle remain visually legible as controls in native-size captures. The added top-right slogan, document footer sentence, intermediate encouragement, conversation header, context row, and bottom disclaimer make this the busiest composer. On narrow screens the intermediate encouragement consumes space without helping the interaction. Its extra explanatory copy is not a quality bonus. No clipping of its primary composer controls was observed in reviewed captures.
- **E, third:** A clean collapsed pill, good document title hierarchy, restrained neutral paper, and a clearly distinct send button. Expanded editing is less balanced: expand/send controls occupy the right side of the textarea row, visibly narrowing the writing area compared with A/C, especially at 390 px. The green outline plus shadow plus inner field border makes focused editing a little more layered. Helper text is small and faint. The no-preference Escape-stage capture remains expanded where A/C are collapsed; that is an observed state, not by itself proof of the initiating event or its cause.

## Picker observations

- **D, preferred:** The nested view has the clearest compact hierarchy: Back/title and current-color preview, labeled name input, two-row color grid, and a visible Create button. At 390 px the complete surface fits comfortably in the viewport. Left alignment is consistent across project context, trigger, and open surface. The rounded outer shell, smaller input/color radii, light dividers, and restrained shadow are coherent. Blue keyboard focus is unambiguous but visually quite strong; that is a trade-off rather than a defect. The peripheral project header and low-contrast helper are expendable. This preference does not depend on the extra project decoration.
- **F, second:** Best scope restraint and closest to the small, label-led reference composition: selected chips, Labels trigger, and a compact picker without a large project heading. Named vertical color rows are easy to scan and the selected checkmark is clear. However, the tall nested form is a real layout weakness: in `F-390-reduce/10-keyboard-color-focus.png`, Create label is cut by the bottom viewport edge. In `F-960-reduce/05-nested-create.png`, the nested shell clips almost all of the bottom action even though space remains below the shell. These reduced-motion captures confirm this is not merely an entrance-opacity sample. Extra chips wrap Labels onto another line and lower the nested surface further. Some no-preference captures show the helper sentence through the entering surface and pale chips; these are transient captured phases, not established persistent contrast defects.
- **B, third with limited visual evidence:** The open search panel has tidy row spacing, a readable focused search input, aligned checkbox/color/name rows, and a clear create affordance. Warm neutral surfaces and green-gray focus styling are pleasant. Its large headline and supporting paragraph overemphasize context for a small picker; narrow-screen body wrapping leaves the word “idea” isolated. More importantly for judging the complete brief, the supplied sequence does not expose a usable nested create view. I cannot fairly score the unseen nested styling or assume it is visually worse than F. B's placement reflects weak evidence of a coherent complete interaction as well as its less focused initial composition, not a claim that every B static surface is inferior.

## Motion and interaction evidence, kept separate

I viewed image samples extracted from real recordings: all six 390 px no-preference videos at 2 fps, plus their first 2.4 seconds at 10 fps. These show A/C progressing through intermediate composer heights while the document stays anchored. E likewise has intermediate expanded geometry. They support a spatial-continuity observation, not a real-time smoothness or easing verdict. The runner resets pages between cases; changes across resets must not be attributed to application motion. B's sequence includes apparent page movement, whose cause is not established here.

D/F picker samples include partially transparent entry states and stable opaque states. Reduced-motion screenshots provide additional settled-looking comparisons. No persistent low-opacity defect is inferred from the filtered no-preference stills. F's list/creation surface is readable once visible; its action clipping remains in reduced-motion captures. The material supplied does not support a confident subjective motion winner. Continuous perceived smoothness, interruption feel, native typing latency, and touch-keyboard behavior remain unverified by this review.

After freezing the visual order, I read `results.json`:

- A and C each pass all nine recorded checks in each of four configurations. This supports draft preservation, the tested keyboard/send paths, synthetic IME suppression, and the runner's reduced-motion checks. It does not establish a complete accessibility audit or native OS IME correctness.
- D and F each pass all eleven checks in each configuration. F therefore illustrates why passing behavior checks does not eliminate the visible nested-form/action clipping.
- E passes eight of nine per configuration: the no-preference failures are in the collapse/Escape/outside/draft sequence, reporting expanded state where collapsed was expected; reduced-motion failures report zero user messages where click-send expected one. I did not inspect source or independently reproduce the root cause.
- B passes five of eleven per configuration. Failures include a search-field timeout in the persistence sequence, missing visible name input when entering nested creation, rapid-switch state mismatch, and keyboard selection producing zero rather than one selected item. Several later cases fail at the same nested-entry prerequisite; these must not be portrayed as six independently diagnosed defects. The results explain why the nested view is unavailable in this visual evidence, but do not establish a source-level diagnosis.

## Confidence and limits

Moderate confidence in the composer order, with C/A close and stylistic discretion substantial. Moderate-to-high confidence that D has a more robust visible nested layout than F at the supplied sizes. Lower confidence in a purely aesthetic F/B order because B's nested design was not exposed. No numerical margins or universal quality claim are warranted.

Common reference contact sheets were inspected. The reference README states composer.mp4 is empty; it was not treated as motion evidence. Native-size selected screenshots supplemented contact sheets. The review did not inspect every one of the 198 captures individually, play recordings continuously, test other viewports/browsers, perform contrast measurements, or exercise the UI itself. Reduced-motion support is backed by the runner checks, not inferred solely from stills. No condition mapping was learned. No implementation/repository writes, commits, or pushes were performed; only the requested report and review contact sheets were written.
